# Wide screens are the same app, laid out by room and designed by role

Kamosu on a tablet or a computer is the phone's app, the same installed PWA, laid out again when the window has room. There is no tablet app and no desktop app. Which layout a window gets depends on its size alone, and how much design a screen gets depends on what each device is for. Settled with Aurélien on 2026-09-28, in a grilling session held before any wide-screen code existed. Until then every screen was a 672px column centred in whatever window it was given.

## What was decided

- **One PWA everywhere.** Installed from Safari on an iPad, from Chrome on an Android tablet, and from Chrome on a computer (in a tab or as an installed app window). Native apps were never on the table once this was settled: they would bring packaging, signing and updates, and a second codebase to keep level with the Catalogue.
- **The layout goes by room, not by device.** The wide layout needs about **700 wide and about 560 tall**. The height half is what keeps a phone turned sideways (about 850 by 390) on the phone layout. Every tablet clears both lines in either orientation, so turning a tablet on its stand never moves the navigation. A small 7–8" tablet in portrait (about 600 wide) gets the phone layout, which suits it. These figures are starting values, to be fitted against real devices the way #88 fitted the cooking screen. Nothing reads the user agent or asks what device it is on.
- **Each device has a role, and the role decides which screens get real design.** The tablet is the kitchen device: cooking, and picking dinner from the couch. The computer is the desk device: importing, editing and tidying. The phone keeps shopping and quick look-ups. Every device keeps every feature. The role only decides which screens get designed properly for the wide layout and which get a sensible default.
- **A sidebar replaces the tab bar** on the wide layout: Home, Recipes, Shopping, Cooked and Settings down the left edge, like Apple's Mail and Notes.
- **Browsing screens open things as full pages; tidying screens keep the list beside what you opened.** Home, Recipes and Cooked stay full pages with wider photo grids, because dinner is picked by looking at photographs and a narrow list column would shrink them. Foods and Imports go side by side, because there you work down a list: open, fix, next.
- **A recipe puts its Ingredients beside its Method** once there is room (landscape tablet and computer). The editor follows, since #83 made the page and the editor the same layout.
- **A page you reach from another page carries a back arrow at its top left** on the wide layout: a recipe, a Food, an import report, a recipe's history, Share. The sidebar's own destinations carry none. The arrow does exactly what the device's back does, so it restores a search as [#191](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/191) makes back do. It exists for the iPad: an app installed from Safari shows no browser buttons (Apple, WWDC23 "What's new in web apps"), and developer reports say Safari's edge swipe does not carry over, so without it the iPad has no reliable way back. Chrome's installed window, a Chrome tab and Android's system back already cover the other devices.
- **Every bottom sheet becomes a window in the middle of the screen** on the wide layout, the page dimmed behind it.
- **The cooking screen keeps ADR 0011 and gets no sidebar.** ADR 0011 rejected the full ingredient list pinned beside the Step for having nine lines of which one matters, and a bigger screen doesn't change that. The screen is made for distance instead, since a tablet on the counter is read from further away than a phone in the hand. Whether it also shows the next Step small beside the current one is decided on prototypes.
- **The keyboard is used where work repeats.** Hover feedback, Escape, Enter and Tab everywhere; up and down arrows through the side-by-side lists; `/` to the recipe search. No letter shortcuts. These work wherever a keyboard and pointer are attached, a Magic Keyboard on an iPad included. Button sizes stay at 48px on every device.
- **Files can be dropped where they have one meaning.** A PDF, a Bundle or a web link onto Recipes; a Crouton export where its import lives; a photograph onto the photo it would set. Nowhere else accepts a drop, so a drop never has to guess.
- **Everything else gets a default.** Shopping puts the chosen recipes on the left and the combined list on the right (ADR 0024 already treats the list as a choice of recipes). Settings, Operator, Thread, Share and the Divergence view become one column at reading width. Sign-in, invites, recovery and About stay centred with no sidebar.

## Considered options

- **Same jobs everywhere, every screen designed equally for the wide layout.** Rejected: twenty-odd screens designed with equal care, when four of them are where each device earns its place.
- **Separate native apps.** Rejected before the rest was discussed, for the costs above.
- **Width alone decides the layout.** Rejected on the sideways phone.
- **Side by side on every list screen, Recipes included.** Rejected because the list column would shrink the photographs. It would suit fixing many recipes in a row at the computer, which is the use it gives up.
- **Bottom tabs kept, or tabs across the top.** Rejected: the first looks like a phone on a 14" screen, and the second is at the edge of a propped-up tablet furthest from the hand.
- **Panels sliding in from the right instead of centred windows.** Rejected so that every sheet behaves the same way. None of them needs the recipe readable while it is open.

## Consequences

- **Four things get prototypes before they are built:** the sidebar, the recipe page and editor in two columns, Foods side by side (the example for the tidying pattern), and the cooking screen with and without the next Step. What Aurélien chooses is added here.
- **Live acceptance runs at fixed sizes:** iPad mini and 11" iPad in both orientations, a 10" Android tablet, a sideways phone, a 14" MacBook at 1512 × 982, and a large monitor. What only a real device shows is checked on Aurélien's iPad Pro 11" (Safari, installed) and MacBook Pro 14" (Chrome).
- **Nothing stored changes.** This is layout over the same Operations. Under [ADR 0009](./0009-the-v1-cut-line-is-drawn-at-shape-not-at-features.md) it is computed on top and can be redesigned again without touching a recipe.

## What the prototypes settled

- **The sidebar is a narrow indigo rail** ([#194](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/194), 5 October 2026). It is 88 wide and full height. The mark sits at its top with Kamosu's name under it, then Home, Recipes, Shopping and Cooked, each an icon over its uppercase label as the tab bar draws them, and Settings at the foot. The header and its *You* card are not drawn on the wide layout, so the rail is the one way to Settings there. Aurélien chose it over a 240-wide paper sidebar holding the name and the *You* card, and over keeping the phone's header above a 208-wide sidebar. His reasons were the band of colour and the width: the rail leaves 746 of an upright iPad Pro 11" for the page, where the others left 594 and 626. The first rail showed the mark alone, and he asked for the name to be written somewhere. Written down the rail and kept in a header above the page were the two other places offered for it. The pages the wide layout draws with no rail (signing in, a recovery link, a Cookbook Invite, a Share Link's import) carry the mark with the name under it, centred above the page, which he asked for the same day. [#214](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/214) gives the phone and the wide layout one signed-out page.
- **The back arrow is a bare round button in the page's top left corner** ([#195](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/195), 5 October 2026). It holds an arrow and no word, sits just right of the rail, and stays there while the page scrolls. Aurélien chose it over a line naming the parent ("‹ Recipes") at the top of the page's column. It can be reached from the foot of a long recipe. It also names nothing, so it cannot name the wrong place for a cook who opened the recipe from Home or Cooked, where the named line would have said Recipes and then gone elsewhere. Where the window leaves no margin beside the page's column, as on an upright tablet, the page starts below the button. Foods, Brought in and the Operator screen carry the arrow too, back to Settings, since the rail lists none of them. On the wide layout the arrow takes the place of the small "‹ Settings" line that Brought in and an import Report draw on the phone.
- **A sheet is a small window in the middle of the room** ([#196](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/196), 5 October 2026). It is 480 wide, as tall as what it holds and never more than four fifths of the window or 704, whichever is less, centred both ways beside the rail, with a soft shadow. The page behind is dimmed as lightly as the phone dims it. Aurélien chose it over a window 672 wide, the width of the page's column, hung from a fixed line near the top over a much darker page. A confirmation is two sentences and two buttons, and at 672 the buttons stretched far past what they said. The lighter dimming keeps the recipe readable while its tags are picked. The cost was shown and accepted: a centred window shrinks around its middle when a list inside gets shorter, so the Tag sheet's search box moves down as a word is typed. One frame draws every sheet on both layouts, and it also closes on Escape, moves the caret in and back, and confirms on Enter where a sheet has one thing to confirm. A Step's photograph is the exception to the window: it covers the whole screen on every layout, since it is opened to be seen large. History's two sheets were not among the twelve the ticket counted and became windows with the rest. On the cooking screen the window is the dark panel that screen's sheets always were, and the Step behind it is dimmed, which the phone does not do.
- **The cooking screen is set larger, and shows the next Step small** ([#197](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/197), 5 October 2026). On the wide layout the Step is 38px where the phone's is 26, the amounts 27px where the phone's are 21, and Back and Next are 72 tall where the phone's are 48. The next Step is written in small grey type: under the Step on an upright tablet, held to three lines, and beside it in three tenths of the width where the window is roomy. The last Step shows none, and nor does a Step the cook is writing on. Aurélien chose this over the same screen without the next Step. Its cost was measured and shown first. Of the library's 591 Steps, each with its own amounts, 95% fit without scrolling on an upright iPad Pro 11" and 91% on one on its side, where the next Step's column takes width from the Step. It is the next Step's words only. The amounts above are still the ones this Step uses, so ADR 0011 holds. Where the window is roomy the amounts sit in two columns, because five of them in one took almost half of a tablet on its side. The screen stops widening at 1120 and sits in the middle of a larger window. The windows the cooking screen opens are set larger with it. The phone's cooking screen, a sideways phone's included, is what it was.
- **A recipe is two columns where the window is roomy, and its Ingredients stay in view** ([#198](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/198), 5 October 2026). The page's column grows from 672 to 1040. The photograph, the facts and the Tags run across the top. Under them the Ingredients take two fifths and the Method three fifths, and the Ingredients stay on screen while the Method scrolls. Where they are taller than the window, their column scrolls on its own. What follows the Method (Related recipes, Cooked, the buttons) goes back to one 672 column in the middle. The editor takes the same layout, since the editor is the page. An upright tablet and a phone keep the one column they had. Aurélien chose this over two even halves with the photograph beside the facts, where both columns scroll away, and over a third-wide Ingredients column with the Method starting under the photograph, where most Ingredient Lines wrapped onto two lines on a tablet. On a tablet on its side the wider column leaves no margin for the back arrow, so the page starts below the arrow there, as it does on an upright one. In the first prototype the Ingredients stayed for the whole page and covered everything under the Method, which he found on the iPad. They are held to the height of the Method, and a screen test fails if that is undone. A line dragged to the edge of the Ingredients' column scrolls that column and leaves the page where it is. A recipe read against another Branch stays one column at reading width, as #192 asked of the Divergence view.
- **Foods keeps a slim list beside the open Food, which is a card** ([#199](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/199), 6 October 2026). The list is 300 wide and as tall as the window. Its title and search box stay at its top and its rows scroll under them. A row is the Food's name with the number of lines that point at it at the right, and the open Food's row has a pale ground and an indigo edge. The open Food is a white card no wider than 600, in the middle of what the list leaves. Before a Food is open, the right side says to choose one. Aurélien chose this from three prototypes, taking the first and adding the number and the card of the third. The second kept the phone's rows, which need a list 420 wide and would have left an upright iPad Pro 11" a Food 326 wide, so it went side by side only where the window is roomy. The third drew the phone's Foods page until the first Food was open and then moved the list to the left. At 300 the list is beside the Food on every wide window, an upright tablet included, where the Food has 446. The back arrow stands over the list's top corner with the list's title to its right. The up and down arrows open the Food above or below in place of the open one, so going back afterwards is one step. A click on a row adds an entry, as any link does, so back after a click and then some arrows returns to the list with no Food open. The arrows are the caret's own while it is in a field. A Food corrected on its card changes its row at once. The phone keeps its two full pages and its rows. This is the pattern [#200](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/200) gives Imports.
- **Brought in keeps a list of every import beside the open Report, which is a card** ([#200](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/200), 6 October 2026). On the phone Brought in lists the three sources and a Report is reached through its source's screen. A list that stays beside a Report has to list the imports themselves, so on the wide layout it does: a heading for each source with what it remembers, and under it each time something came in, as the date and time with what happened under it in the words the source's screen uses. A run that brought nothing is in red. The heading leads to the source's screen, which stays a full page on every layout and is still the only place forgetting is offered. The list is 300 wide and the open Report is a white card no wider than 600, as on Foods, and the list of everything that arrived stays behind its disclosure. Aurélien chose this from two prototypes. The other was one run of every import, newest first, with the number of recipes at the right of each row, the sources as links at the foot of the list, and a Report with no card that grew to 900 and opened its long list into columns. The arrows walk the list as they walk Foods, and pass from one source's imports to the next. A Report that watches its import end tells the list, whose row for it was read while it ran. The phone keeps its three full pages.
- **Home, Recipes and Cooked run to the window's edge, with cards the size the phone's are** ([#201](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/201), 6 October 2026). The page starts at the rail and its title stays on the left. On Recipes the grid takes as many columns as fit with no card narrower than 160: 4 across on an upright iPad Pro 11", 6 on its side, 8 on a MacBook 14". The search box stops at 672 and the filters stay under it. Home's shelves are still sideways rails that run off the right edge, and their cards keep the phone's 168. On Cooked a month is a grid of cards no narrower than 224, which is 3, 4 and 5 across, and a cooking opens inside its own card. Cards in a row share a height until one of them is open. Aurélien chose this over cards at least 224 wide on Recipes and Home and 320 on Cooked, which gave 3, 4 and 5 recipes across. The larger cards showed one and a half of Home's shelves on an iPad on its side before scrolling, and these show two. An iPad's screen is also coarser than a phone's, so a card of the same size is a little larger in the hand. The grid keeps adding columns on a large monitor, 14 across at 2560. He was shown that and offered a page that stops widening, and did not ask for it. The phone's three screens are what they were.
- **Shopping is two columns on one page, with − and + on each recipe** ([#202](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/202), 6 October 2026). The page is as wide as a recipe's, 1040, with the title across the top. The recipes chosen take two fifths and stay in view while what to buy scrolls in the other three, in one column. Each recipe has − and + either side of how much of it is being shopped for, so one more is one tap, and the amount between them still opens the two boxes. They are drawn where the recipe's Yield is a whole number, and step as the recipe page's do. A recipe with no Yield, one like "1½ loaves", and one being shopped for in another noun than its own ("2 trays" of a recipe written for "4 servings") keep the sentence that opens the boxes. A recipe that has moved from what it is written for says so under its − and +. The amount changes as it is tapped, before the Core answers, so two quick taps are two servings. In a column this narrow Save and Cancel go under the two boxes. Aurélien chose this over the recipes in a column 300 wide that stays put, as the Foods list does, with the phone's sentence under each name and the list running in two columns where there was room. Unlike a recipe, Shopping is two columns on an upright tablet too, where the recipes get about 270. On an iPad mini upright they get 234, and "Take off" goes under the − and +. "Take off the list" is "Take off" on the wide layout. The − and + are circles 40 across with a quiet outline, as the prototype drew them. They were first built square and 48 like the recipe page's, and Aurélien asked for the prototype's back. With nothing chosen the recipes' side still stands and says what the list is for, since a column that came and went would move the list across the page. The phone's screen is what it was.
- **Under the pointer the paper darkens** ([#203](https://github.com/BattermanZ/Kamosu/issues/203), 6 October 2026). Whatever is under the pointer changes shade and nothing else. An outlined button, a tag, a filter or a list row fills with the darker paper. An indigo button turns a lighter indigo, which is the one colour this added to the palette. A text link gets a patch of the darker paper a little wider than its words. A recipe's card brightens. A place on the sidebar gets a faint patch of paper and its words turn to the paper's colour, and the current place stays as it is. On the cooking screen the shade is that room's panel indigo. The pointer is a hand over anything that can be clicked, and the change fades in over 120ms. Aurélien chose this over an indigo line (an indigo border, a thicker underline, an indigo frame round a card), because an indigo border already says "this one is chosen" and a line drawn in passing read as that. All of it applies only where the pointer can hover, so an iPad shows none of it until a trackpad is attached. It is written once in `ui/src/app.css` for every control, and no screen spells out a hover state of its own. The story is the one exception, since it draws itself in plain CSS that the stylesheet does not reach, and it says the same shades again. A tick box's label gets the hand and no shade. A control that lies over a photograph gets neither. `/` goes to the recipe search wherever the navigation is drawn, and not from a field, with a sheet open, on the cooking screen, or on a page read before signing in. A French keyboard writes `/` with Shift, so Shift is let through.
